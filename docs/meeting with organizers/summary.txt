The conversation focused on the technical requirements and evaluation criteria for a regulation-matching system, specifically applied to the materials and assembly of a "rail lamp." The goal is to develop a solution that identifies relevant regulatory passages based on product descriptions and material lists.

Core System Requirements
• Input Data: The system must process an "element book" containing the five parts of the rail lamp and their constituent materials (e.g., plastic, glass).
• Regulatory Scope: The task is not to dictate how to build the product (e.g., windshield material) but to identify regulations governing the delivery and performance of that product (e.g., defrosting speed).
• Granularity: It is insufficient to simply find a regulation; the system must highlight the specific relevant section, page, and paragraph.
• Output Format: Each requirement must include an ID, the original text from the regulation, and the source citation (version, page, and paragraph).

Performance & Evaluation
• Metric Weighting (F2 Score): The evaluation prioritizes Recall over Precision. In a regulatory context, missing a requirement is considered more critical than including a redundant one.
• Validation: A "code looper" script is provided to validate the format, precision, and recall of the output, though it does not check the semantic accuracy of the text itself.
• Architecture Preference: If two solutions achieve similar scores, preference will be given to "lighter" models that can be deployed locally on user computers without heavy AI infrastructure.

Technical Strategy
• Step-by-Step Processing: Avoid "one-shot" generation. The recommended approach is to first generate requirements and then refine them based on the specific platform or engineering constraints.
• Contextual Linking: Regulations often link to other documents. The system should ideally navigate these links to provide a comprehensive view of the assembly requirements.
• Material Constraints: The current model assumes parts "clip" together (e.g., knobs), noting that fasteners like screws are currently omitted from the primary parts list.
